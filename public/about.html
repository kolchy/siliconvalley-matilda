About Us

House music was born in Chicago in the early 1980s, when DJs like Frankie Knuckles at the Warehouse club began blending disco, soul and European electronic music with drum machines like the Roland TR-808 and TR-909. The name "house" is widely said to come from the Warehouse itself, where Knuckles' marathon sets defined the sound.

By the mid-1980s, Chicago producers such as Marshall Jefferson, Larry Heard (Mr. Fingers) and Jesse Saunders were releasing raw, machine-driven tracks that spread to New York, Detroit and across the Atlantic. In the UK, house music ignited the acid house movement and the rave scene of the late 1980s, soundtracking the "Second Summer of Love" in 1988.

Since then, house has splintered into countless subgenres, from deep house and tech house to acid, progressive and electro, and its four-on-the-floor pulse now underpins much of modern dance and pop music. What started in a Chicago club has become one of the most influential sounds in the world.
